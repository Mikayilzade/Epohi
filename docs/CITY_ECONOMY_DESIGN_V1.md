# City & economy design V1

Status: **ACCEPTED CORE DIRECTION — details still open**

## 1. What a city is

A city is the primary economic and production engine of a civilization.

Without a city, the civilization effectively has no normal economic base:
- no stable resource income;
- no place to train regular military units;
- no place to construct buildings;
- no developed infrastructure for science, gold and other economic outputs.

A city is therefore not just a map object or territorial marker. It is the place where land, population and infrastructure are converted into useful outputs.

## 2. Why additional cities matter

A second city is a real strategic advantage because it creates another economic and production center.

More cities can provide:
- more total resources;
- more production capacity;
- more places to train units;
- more buildings/infrastructure;
- more potential science output;
- more potential gold output;
- more specialization options.

The intended strategic consequence is that expansion can accelerate development.

A player may use an additional city to:
- build military strength faster;
- create a science-focused center;
- create a gold/economic center;
- support wider territorial development;
- combine several roles.

## 3. Core city fantasy

The city should feel like:

> a local engine that turns territory + population + infrastructure into resources, units and long-term civilization growth.

The player should have a clear reason to care about where a city is founded and how it is developed.

## 4. Important design implication

If every additional city only gives "more of everything" with no meaningful cost, limitation or specialization pressure, expansion may become an automatic dominant choice.

This is not yet solved.

Possible future balancing levers include:
- founding cost;
- settler cost;
- population requirements;
- time to become productive;
- local resource constraints;
- administration/maintenance;
- specialization trade-offs;
- defense burden;
- distance/logistics.

No such limiter is accepted yet.

## 5. Next question

Decide resource ownership model:

**Which outputs are local to each city, and which belong to the empire-wide pool?**

This must be decided before formulas for city income, buildings and growth are finalized.
